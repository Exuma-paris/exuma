Subject: two Patagonian baqueanos riding across the open pampa at the foot of the Torres del Paine, Chilean Patagonia. An original documentary travel photograph.

The riders: two working Chilean horsemen, not models and not a folkloric troupe. Seen from BEHIND and slightly to one side as they ride away from the camera — we read their backs, their shoulders and the brims of their hats. NO frontal portrait, NO faces turned to the lens. They sit deep and relaxed in the saddle, reins in one hand, the posture of men who ride every day. Authentic working dress worn soft with use: flat-brimmed dark felt hats with chin cords, heavy wool ponchos or faded canvas jackets flattened against their backs by the wind, bombacha trousers gathered into worn leather boots, a coiled lasso at one saddle. Nothing new, nothing clean, no bright modern outdoor gear.

The horses: two criollos, the Chilean-Argentine working breed — compact and muscular, short strong back, deep chest, thick neck, short sturdy legs, small alert head with a straight profile, around 1.45 m at the withers. Definitely NOT tall sport horses. Coats in bay and dun, one with a dark dorsal stripe. CRITICAL on the coats: they must NOT look smooth, clean or computer-generated. Short hair but visibly textured, whorls and cowlicks over the flank and hindquarters, muscle and tendon standing out under the skin, veins showing on the shoulder, longer tufts under the belly and at the fetlocks, dried dust and mud on the legs and hocks, a sweat and girth mark under the saddle, manes and tails uneven and tangled, blowing sideways in the wind. Authentic working tack: sheepskin-topped recado saddles, plaited rawhide reins, simple bits, saddlebags.

Movement: they are at a walk or an easy trot through the tussock grass, one horse half a length ahead of the other, dust lifting faintly from the hooves. Caught mid-stride, not standing still and posed.

Setting: wide open Patagonian pampa in dry ochre and straw coirón tussock grass, low thorny shrubs, a few bleached bare lenga trunks, a shallow rise crossed by an old fence line running off to nowhere. Behind and above, the dark granite and shale of the Paine massif with the three towers standing clear, snow in the high couloirs, softened by distance and a little haze.

Light and weather: late afternoon, an hour before sunset. Low warm raking side light skimming the grass and catching the horses' flanks and the riders' shoulders, long soft shadows across the pampa. High wind-drawn cirrus in a wide sky, cloud caught on the summits. The Patagonian wind visible in the grass, the manes and the ponchos.

Camera: eye level, standing in the grass thirty metres away — never aerial, never a drone. Around 85 to 135 mm, shot from a respectful distance so the massif reads large and compressed behind the riders. Deep enough focus that the towers stay legible; the very near foreground grass slightly soft. Visible 35 mm film grain.

Composition: CRITICAL — this frame will be cropped to a CENTRED SQUARE on the website. Place the two riders squarely in the middle of the frame, with the towers rising directly above and behind them and grass below. Nothing that matters may sit near the left or right edges: those sides will be cut away.

No vehicles, no buildings, no tourists, no signage, no logos, no text.
