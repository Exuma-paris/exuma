Subject: a Rapa Nui guide standing before the moai of Ahu Tongariki, Easter Island, early morning, the site still empty of visitors. Documentary travel photography, an original frame.

The man: a Rapanui guide in his forties — Polynesian features, brown skin, thick dark hair tied back or cut short, solid build. Everyday local clothes, nothing costumed and nothing branded: a plain dark t-shirt or a light open shirt, worn trousers, a small canvas shoulder bag. He stands on the grass a few metres from the platform, hands at his sides or one hand resting on his bag, looking up at the statues.

CRITICAL — he is seen from BEHIND, three-quarters back to the camera. We read his shoulders, the back of his head and the line of his jaw at most. NO frontal portrait, NO facial features presented to the lens, NO eye contact. He is small in the frame, roughly a quarter of its height, dwarfed by the statues — a man at the foot of the ahu, not a subject posing for a photograph.

The moai: the long line of fifteen statues of Ahu Tongariki standing shoulder to shoulder on their stone platform, backs to the ocean, facing inland toward the camera and the man. Massive figures of grey volcanic tuff, heavy brows, long straight noses, elongated ears, arms held flat against the body, hands meeting low over the belly. Their surfaces are pitted, lichen-stained and weathered, each one slightly different in height and tilt; one wears a red scoria topknot. The ahu itself is a low wall of dark basalt blocks, with a wide apron of rounded volcanic stones spread in front of it.

Setting and time: just after sunrise, the sun already clear of the horizon behind the statues and off to one side. Coarse green and straw-coloured coastal grass in the foreground, the dark volcanic coast and the Pacific behind the platform, the slope of a green headland in the distance. The site is completely deserted — no other visitors, no tour group, no vehicles, no barriers, no signage.

Light: low warm morning light raking across the ahu from the side, modelling the stone and throwing long shadows across the grass toward the camera. Some of the statues catch the light on one cheek and stay dark on the other. Soft haze in the air off the sea. Warm ochre, olive green, deep desaturated blue; dense shadows, nothing washed out.

Camera: eye level, standing on the grass fifteen or twenty metres back — never aerial. Around 35 to 50 mm, natural perspective, deep enough focus that both the man and the statues read clearly. Visible 35 mm film grain.

Composition: CRITICAL — this frame will be cropped hard to a TALL 3:4 VERTICAL on the website, so build it for that crop. Place the man and the nearest few moai squarely in the centre of the frame, with clear sky above the statues and grass below the man. Nothing that matters may sit near the left or right edges: those sides will be cut away.

No other people, no logos, no text, no signage.
