Subject: a Patagonian baqueano — a working Chilean horseman — standing with his criollo horses in front of an estancia in the Torres del Paine region, Chilean Patagonia. Documentary travel photography, an original frame.

The man: a real working rider in his fifties, weathered face, seen three-quarters from behind and slightly to the side so he reads as a person at work rather than a poser for the camera. Authentic Patagonian gaucho dress: flat-brimmed dark felt hat with a chin cord, thick wool poncho or a worn canvas jacket over a plain shirt, wide leather belt, bombacha trousers gathered into soft leather boots. He holds the reins of one horse loosely in his hand and rests the other hand on its neck. No costume look, no folkloric staging, nothing new or clean — leather worn smooth, cloth faded by the wind.

The horses: two or three criollos, the Argentine-Chilean working breed — compact and muscular, short strong back, deep chest, thick neck, short sturdy legs, small alert head with a straight profile. Roughly 1.45 m at the withers, definitely not a tall sport horse. Coats in bay, dun and buckskin with a dark dorsal stripe. Fitted with authentic working tack: a sheepskin-topped recado saddle, plaited rawhide reins, simple bit. One horse stands quietly in profile beside the man, the others a little behind, one with its head lowered.

Setting: the packed earth and grass yard in front of a low estancia building — corrugated metal roof gone matte with age, weathered wood-plank walls, a small covered porch, a rough wooden post-and-rail corral running off to one side. Behind and above it, the Patagonian pampa in dry ochre tussock grass, then the dark granite ridges of the Paine massif with snow in the high couloirs, softened by distance.

Light and weather: late afternoon, an hour before sunset. Low warm side light raking across the yard, catching the horses' coats and the man's shoulders, long soft shadows on the ground. High wind-drawn cirrus cloud. The Patagonian wind visible in the horses' manes and in the grass.

Camera: eye level, standing in the yard about ten metres away — never aerial or drone. Around 50 to 85 mm, natural perspective, shallow-ish depth of field so the estancia is present but softer than the man and horses.
