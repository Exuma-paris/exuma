Subject: a small private sailing schooner lying at anchor in front of the Serrano glacier, in the Kawésqar fjords of Chilean Patagonia. An original travel photograph.

The boat: a modest expedition schooner, twenty to twenty-five metres, of the kind that works these channels — a dark navy or deep green steel hull streaked with rust at the anchor hawse, a white or cream deckhouse, two masts with sails furled on the booms, a radar arch, a grey inflatable tender made fast alongside. Clearly a working boat kept in good order, NOT a white charter yacht and NOT a cruise ship. She lies broadside to the camera, motionless, her anchor chain running down into still water, her hull and rig reflected almost perfectly on the surface. She sits small in the frame — perhaps a fifth of its width — so the scale of the ice behind her reads properly.

The glacier: a tidewater glacier flowing down a hanging valley and calving into the fjord behind the boat. A high fractured wall of ice at the water, riven with deep seracs and crevasses, its blue coming from within the ice rather than from the sky. Grey-black moraine rubble along its flanks, waterfalls streaking the rock walls on either side, and above it a snowfield running up into low cloud. Scattered growlers and small brash ice drift on the water between the boat and the ice front.

Setting: sheer dark granite walls closing the fjord on both sides, their lower slopes hung with dense green sub-antarctic forest — twisted lenga and coigüe, soaked and wind-flattened. Low grey cloud sitting on the summits. Water almost flat, milky green-grey with glacial flour, a slight breath of wind ruffling one patch of it.

Light and weather: overcast Patagonian daylight late in the afternoon, soft and directionless, with one weak break in the cloud letting a warmer shaft touch the boat and the near water while the glacier stays cool and blue. Damp air, a hint of mist over the ice. Palette of deep desaturated blue-green, wet granite grey, moss and warm boat tones. Dense shadows, nothing washed out or pastel.

Camera: eye level, low over the water as if shot from the tender a few hundred metres off — never aerial, never a drone. Around 35 to 50 mm, natural perspective, everything in focus from the boat to the ice. Visible 35 mm film grain.

Realism: a genuine reportage frame — the boat unposed, the water and cloud caught as they were, no dramatic sunset, no glossy postcard treatment.
